Bottom line

Yes — and "pulling timing" is only one of four things

  1. Four mechanisms make the Z slower when hot, and timing retard is probably not the biggest one on this car. Likely order of impact: air density (at the 130–145 °F intake temps the box logged, 5–7 % is gone before the ECU acts); oil-temp "engine protection mode" (Nissan documents it for MY2009–2019; the FSM says the ECM restricts VVEL lift and lowers the rev limit — owners see a cap of ≈6,000–6,500 rpm around 280 °F on the gauge, then ≈3,500–4,500); ignition retard from knock feedback and temperature-correction tables (the "pulling timing" part); and transmission protection keyed to ATF temperature.

  2. It can be built into the app with the tap you already have — no new hardware for the core. The box already reads timing advance (PID 0x0E), IAT, ECT, load, throttle, rpm, baro and — in v6.14 — oil temp via Mode 22. What's missing is how timing is sampled, a baseline to compare against, and two cheap signals readable over the same wire: pedal position and the TCM's own ATF temperature.

  3. Today's timing channel cannot see a heat pull. Timing updates once every 3–5 s; a pull gets 2–3 samples paired with load values up to 3 s stale. The existing pen_timing penalty fires in 24.5 % of rows of a 12-minute street drive with six short pulls, and in 9 % of rows of a drive that never passed 35 % throttle. It measures operating-point changes, not retard.

  4. The design (Part 3): burst-poll timing at ≈8 Hz only during power windows; learn the car's own cool-morning timing map per rpm bin; show Δtiming against it; add rev-ceiling, pedal-vs-throttle-gap and load-deficit detectors plus an air-density readout so "hot air" and "ECU pulling" are separated; surface it as an event banner, a POWER popup, appended CSV columns and a per-lap/session verdict.

  5. What else (Part 4): a fuel-pressure sender on spare AI6 (catches the saddle-tank starvation that mimics heat loss, and it is the physical fuel-pressure test already planned for the −12 % rich trims), TCM ATF temps + torque-converter slip (free), pedal/wheel speeds/steering from passive CAN (free), 10–25 Hz GPS for per-lap "how much slower" numbers, a wideband on AI7, a BME280 for real density altitude.

What this means for this car

  • Oil temp is the mechanism Nissan itself documents. 2009 owner's manual: "When the engine oil temperature is high, the engine protection mode, which helps reduce the chance of engine damage, could activate. The engine protection mode automatically decreases engine power." The 2018 and 2019 quick-reference guides repeat it and set 280 °F as the "reduce engine speed" line. The FSM passage (quoted by two independent posters): "To avoid VVEL performance, ECM performs the engine torque cut control at high engine oil temperature… This control is to control the VVEL operating angle… engine performance will decrease, then maximum engine speed is reduced a little." The gauge, the protection logic and DID 0x111F all read the same sensor — the ECM feeds the gauge over CAN.
  • There is currently no oil temp on track. The PRTXI AI2 sensor is dead and v6.14's Mode-22 fallback is not flashed. Flashing v6.14 is prerequisite #1 for everything here.
  • The IAT you log is the bank-1 MAF-housing temperature (the ECM ignores the bank-2 IAT). On 7/24 it ran 97–145 °F with the "ambient" PID reading 70–118 °F — genuine heat soak.
  • Coolant at ~230 °F on 7/6 was not triggering an ECU derate — none is documented — though hot coolant raises knock tendency.
  • Knock counts are out of reach from the box. EcuTek, UpRev and HP Tuners read them as ROM-address "memory parameters"; no public Mode 22 DID exists. Δtiming vs baseline is the observable proxy.

Bus risk to keep in mind. The ESP32 is a node on the powertrain CAN, and the FSM lists CAN-communication-loss DTCs as a trigger for the 3,500 rpm VVEL limp. Keep request rates bounded and never loop bus-off recovery.

Part 3

The Heat Derate Monitor — what exactly, and how it works

3.1 Fast timing sampling in power windows

  • Add a second PID list, OBD_PID_LIST_POWER[] = {0x0E, 0x0C, 0x0E, 0x11, 0x0E, 0x04, 0x0E, 0x0C} at OBD_PID_REQUEST_PERIOD_POWER_MS = 60. Timing ≈ 8 Hz, rpm ≈ 4 Hz, throttle and load ≈ 2 Hz.
  • Enter power mode when throttle ≥ 70 % && rpm ≥ 3000 (or pedal ≥ 80 % once pedal exists); leave 1.5 s after the condition clears. Outside it the existing 26-PID list runs unchanged — temperatures don't move fast.
  • While in power mode, exempt the slow PIDs from the 5 s staleness timeout (or raise it to 10 s) so temperatures don't flicker to "---" on a long straight.
  • Each fast sample is stored with its own millis() and paired with the nearest rpm/load samples (the per-PID timestamps already exist in g_obd_data), so bins are built from aligned values, not from CSV rows.
  • Where: the processOBD() scheduler (g_obd_pid_index, OBD_PID_REQUEST_PERIOD_MS) and decodeOBD_Mode01Reply case 0x0E. Bus load stays ≤ 17 requests/s — inside what ELM-class loggers do.

3.2 A learned cool baseline — the thing pen_timing never had

  • g_tim_base[12]: rpm bins of 500 from 2,000 to 8,000, WOT only (throttle ≥ 70 % && load ≥ 85 %). Each bin keeps an EMA (α ≈ 0.1) and a sample count.
  • A sample updates the baseline only while the engine is cool: oil < 230 °F, IAT < 120 °F, ECT < 205 °F. The first cool laps of the day become the reference. Persisted in Preferences namespace timbase, written once at session end, so it survives reboots and sharpens over track days. Long-press in the POWER popup resets it.
  • Δtiming = live − base[bin], valid once the bin holds ≥ 5 samples. Time-qualified alarm: Δ ≤ −3° held ≥ 1 s → amber; ≤ −6° → red.
  • Attribution from which temperature is elevated at that moment: IAT (> 120 °F), OIL (> 250 °F), ECT (> 215 °F), else KNOCK/FUEL? — retard with no temperature excuse.

3.6 The Fuel Trust fix — same class of bug

computeFuelTrust()'s timing term compares each 0x0E sample with the previous one, 3–5 s and thousands of rpm apart. Replace it with the baseline Δ from 3.2, counted only inside power windows, keeping the 1.5 pts/event, max 30 weighting. This is not a softening — the current term is noise — but it will change the score, so it's flagged as a decision rather than assumed.

3.8 On-car validation plan

  1. Flash v6.14 → confirm the Mode-22 oil-temp offset against the factory gauge. You finally have oil temp on track.
  2. Boot → read the bitmap line; add whichever of 0x49/0x4A/0x4C/0x61–0x63 answer.
  3. At idle → TCM 21 20 dump; match ATF1/ATF2 to the PRTXI trans reading.
  4. First drive → confirm the 0x180 pedal and rpm scale against 0x0C and 0x11.
  5. Track day: the morning session builds the baseline; the afternoon shows Δ. Compare the verdict strings with how the car felt.

3.9 Risks

  • CPU/RAM: negligible — a second PID list, ~1 KB of bins, one popup. Passive sniffing raises RX interrupt load; keep the drain tight.
  • Bus etiquette: ≤ 17 requests/s, watch TWAI alerts, stop on bus-off rather than auto-recover in a loop (FSM: CAN-loss DTC → 3,500 rpm limp).
  • NVS wear: write the baseline at most once per session.
  • The 0x0E absolute scale: unverified but irrelevant to Δ; verify once for the popup's absolute number.
